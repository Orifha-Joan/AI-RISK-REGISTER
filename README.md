# AI-RISK-REGISTER

Most organizations adopt AI without properly evaluating the risks associated with the systems they are introducing.

You might be wondering, **why is AI risk evaluation important?**

AI risks, like every other digital risk, do not only affect the technical team. They can directly impact **business operations, customers, employees, regulatory obligations, finances, and organizational reputation**.

A poorly designed or poorly governed AI system can result in privacy violations, operational disruption, legal consequences, financial losses, reputational damage, and other significant business risks.

Yet, organizations often treat these risks as purely technical problems and leave them entirely to the IT or cybersecurity team.

**This project challenges that approach.**

## About the Project

This project uses a fictional healthcare organization, **The Light Diagnostic Center (T_LDC)**, to demonstrate how organizations can identify, evaluate, and treat risks associated with AI adoption.

T_LDC operates in the healthcare industry and handles sensitive internal and external data, including **personal information and protected health information**.

The organization intends to integrate AI into different areas of its workflow to improve operational efficiency, increase capacity, and support its growing demand.

While management and employees are excited about the potential benefits of AI, the organization must first understand the risks associated with the AI systems it intends to build and deploy.

This project therefore evaluates the proposed AI systems from a **security, operational, business, governance, and patient-safety perspective**.

## Objective

The objective of this project is to demonstrate that:

> **AI adoption should not begin with "What can this technology do?" but with "What risks does this technology introduce, and what controls are required to use it safely and effectively?"**

The assessment identifies potential AI-specific risks, evaluates their likelihood and impact, and considers the potential consequences to T_LDC and its stakeholders.

## Frameworks Used

The assessment draws from established AI security and governance frameworks, including:

* **ISO/IEC 42001** for Artificial Intelligence Management Systems
* **OWASP Top 10 for Large Language Model Applications**
* **NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF)**
* **MITRE ATLAS** for adversarial threats against AI systems

## Key Areas of Assessment

The assessment considers risks across the AI lifecycle, including:

* Sensitive information disclosure
* Prompt injection and adversarial attacks
* Data, model, and knowledge-base poisoning
* Misinformation
* Unbounded consumption
* Excessive agency and autonomous actions
* AI and patient safety
* AI supply-chain vulnerabilities

## Why This Matters

The purpose of this project is not to argue that organizations should avoid AI.

Rather, it demonstrates that **responsible AI adoption requires organizations to understand the relationship between AI capability, risk, controls, and business value.**
